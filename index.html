02 — Practice

Onions have layers.
So do I.

Design and research matter more than ever in the age of AI. The work gets done in new ways, but the fundamental game of delivering value by solving real problems remains the same.

i

Strategic

When AI makes building anything cheap, the real value lies in knowing what to build and what not to build. Strategic design is how you decide. Jobs-to-be-done, innovation frameworks and business canvases define precise, high-value product shapes, and that is more important than ever for standing out from the noise.

ii

Structural

Once you know what to build, structure decides how well it holds together. Service blueprints, design systems and content strategy connect journeys, systems and teams, while AI-assisted workflows make delivery faster.

iii

Surface

This is where the idea becomes real. Prototypes, agents and production interfaces are built alongside engineers, analysts and, increasingly, AI as a pair.

  • 30 / 60 / 90

    Show it early

    Share work at 30%, 60% and 90%. The earlier people see an idea, the cheaper it is to find out it’s wrong.

  • Research

    Evidence beats opinion

    No number of colleagues can stand in for customers. Research is its own discipline, not a bolt-on to UX.

  • Delivery

    Design makes engineering faster

    Good design clarifies the customer need, the outcome that matters and what to actually build.

03 — Selected work

Greater Bank · 2019 – 2022

Building a design practice inside a bank

UX didn’t exist as a formal practice at Greater Bank, a customer‑owned bank rebuilding its entire digital platform. Over about a year we established it and worked out how design should flow into software delivery. The team grew into UX, dedicated design research, content and service design, working alongside engineering rather than underneath it.

One moment made the case for research. Around 1,000 customers across NSW took a survey about money, which produced six personas. When about 50 people from our own product and technology teams took the same survey, nearly all landed on one persona. Three customer segments had no one representing them internally.

We ran five‑day design sprints with customers in the room, sketching concepts alongside the team before anything was tested with the market.
